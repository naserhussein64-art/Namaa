## 2. Short orientation

### Idea

Namaa is a proposed family of non-formal learning and support programmes for children in Syria. It aims to combine educational support, inclusive access, useful life and health knowledge, and developmental opportunities. Qualified specialists would design and review each programme, train Syrian teachers or facilitators, and remain involved in quality assurance. Delivery would take place locally within an accountable and safeguarded framework whose legal form and permission route remain open.

### Working problem

Some children in Syria may lack a reliable, regular, inclusive, and measurable support pathway from access and admission through sustained participation and progress to an appropriate educational connection. The project does not claim that services are absent. Its working concern is that reach, continuity, accessibility, completion, transition, and visible outcomes are not assured for every child. Limited reach of existing offers and administrative barriers are retained as contributing concerns; their relative importance and local effect remain unverified.

This is a project problem definition consistent with the reviewed evidence, not a verified universal finding. The evidence boundary is documented in [Problem landscape and evidence](../modules/Problem%20landscape%20and%20evidence.md).

### Current phase

Namaa is in concept development. It is not an approved programme, operating service, funded commitment, validated solution, designed pilot, or implemented technology.

### Next consequential decision

Namaa project leadership must decide whether this dossier is sufficiently clear to serve as the formal handover baseline for specialists. If accepted, the next selection is the first target group and programme area, followed by appointment of the relevant specialist leads.

### Success for the current phase

The current phase succeeds when specialists can understand the intended problem, target-group frame, objectives, role boundaries, quality principles, open choices, and prohibited claims without having to infer them from conversation history.

## 3. Target-group frame

The retained target groups are listed below. Any barriers described alongside them are evidence-based examples for later specialist consideration, not additional target-group definitions.

1. **Returnee children.** Evidence-reviewed examples include changes in schooling, curriculum, language of instruction, grade placement, or documentation.
2. **Children with disabilities**, including children who need accessible formats, adapted environments, or specialist support.
3. **Children who are not attending school**, irrespective of whether the immediate cause is capacity, poverty, safety, documentation, disability, displacement, or another barrier.
4. **Girls during puberty.** Evidence-reviewed possible concerns include access, health information, safety, family restrictions, and protection.
5. **Children with multi-year education interruptions**, particularly children who missed substantial learning time during the war.

The groups overlap and must not be counted as separate populations. Exact ages, definitions, selection criteria, priority order, and the first subgroup remain open. The evidence is not equally strong for every group; puberty-specific needs have not yet been researched to the same depth as interrupted schooling, return, disability, and non-attendance.

## 4. Objectives

Namaa should:

- give participating children regular and understandable support rather than isolated activities;
- help children develop educational knowledge and skills and remain connected to an appropriate learning path;
- provide useful, age-appropriate knowledge that supports health awareness and practical orientation in life;
- make access and materials inclusive for children with different disabilities and learning needs;
- build local delivery capacity through programme-specific training and continuing support for Syrian teachers or facilitators;
- measure participation, completion, accessibility, progress, usefulness, and implementation quality without inventing impact targets;
- improve programmes through controlled feedback and evidence.

No numerical impact target, cohort size, implementation date, or scale commitment has been approved.

## 5. Programme architecture

Namaa is a modular programme family, not one fixed course. The following are retained candidate domains. Their possible contributions are illustrative questions for specialist review, not settled programme content:

| Domain | Illustrative contribution for specialist review | Current boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| School learning and subject knowledge | Support learning recovery, foundational skills, or continued school participation. | Subjects, age groups, assessment, and sequence must be selected by education specialists. |
| Sport and movement | Support healthy activity, participation, teamwork, and development. | Activities require age-, disability-, safety-, and first-aid review. |
| Coding and digital learning | Develop age-appropriate digital and problem-solving skills. | An academy, platform, device model, or curriculum has not been selected. |
| Physical health and health literacy | Provide understandable information about bodies, health, hygiene, development, and when professional help is needed. | No diagnosis, treatment, psychotherapy, or unrestricted medical advice. |
| Life knowledge and foundational principles | Explore practical understanding, responsibility, relationships, wellbeing, and orientation in everyday life. | Exact topics and principles require transparent specialist and Syrian contextual review. |
| Accessibility and inclusion | Ensure that participation, materials, communication, and assessment work for different needs. | This is a cross-cutting quality requirement, not an optional standalone add-on. |

Game-based and playful learning is a preferred design direction where it supports the learning objective and is appropriate for the child and context. It is not a compulsory method for every programme. Exact pedagogy, content, learning sequence, exercises, and evaluation methods remain the responsibility of the relevant specialists.

## 6. Development and delivery model

The agreed chain is:

> Namaa project concept -> development by qualified specialists -> training of Syrian teachers and facilitators -> delivery with children -> quality assurance and continuous improvement.

The model separates concept ownership from professional content responsibility. Namaa defines purpose, scope, governance, minimum safeguards, documentation, and quality expectations. Specialists determine the professional and pedagogical design within those boundaries. Trained Syrian teachers or facilitators deliver only the programme and version for which they have been prepared and assessed.

Remote training from Germany is the current recommended starting model because the concept relies on German-based professional resources and Syrian local delivery. It is not a permanent or approved delivery decision; specialists, the local implementation framework, authorities where relevant, infrastructure, safety, and participant needs may require another model.

## 7. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YISA/Namaa leadership | Concept ownership, governance, scope, safeguarding system, partner coordination, documentation, funding decisions, and quality oversight. | Does not replace professional judgement or local authority. |
| Subject specialists based in Germany | Develop and review programme content and train adults within their documented competence. | No general authority outside their discipline; volunteer status does not remove accountability. |
| Adult-learning or teacher-development specialists | Design a suitable training and competence-assessment process for Syrian teachers or facilitators. | A participation certificate alone does not authorise work with children. |
| Qualified Syrian professionals and contextual reviewers | Review Arabic language, cultural relevance, feasibility, education context, referral pathways, and local professional boundaries. | Review does not replace organisational permission or formal authority. |
| Syrian teachers and facilitators | Complete programme-specific training and assessment, deliver the approved version, document implementation, and participate in coaching and review. | Must not diagnose, investigate safeguarding allegations, provide treatment, or alter controlled content without approval. |
| Local implementation framework, legal form still open | Provide accountable local coordination, safeguarding arrangements, supervision, referral links, and any permissions required for the selected activity. | No organisation or legal route is confirmed; the exact Syrian requirements remain unverified. |
| Private individuals | May contribute as specialists, trainers, facilitators, or local contacts. | They do not by themselves replace an authorised host, safeguarding structure, or required authority coordination. |

The current model assumes that early specialist contributions may be voluntary. The final appointment, contract, expense, insurance, liability, and availability arrangements have not been decided.

## 8. Binding quality principles

Every programme must follow these principles before it can be considered for child-facing delivery:

1. **Specialist ownership:** Content is developed and reviewed by people with documented competence in the relevant subject and age group.
2. **Child safeguarding:** Safe recruitment, a code of conduct, reporting routes, response procedures, referral links, and clear professional boundaries are required.
3. **Syrian contextual review:** Arabic language, cultural context, education pathways, feasibility, and referral arrangements require qualified local review.
4. **Inclusion by design:** Accessible formats and reasonable adaptations are planned from the beginning.
5. **Complementarity:** Namaa must work with or alongside existing services and must not claim a gap merely because public data is incomplete.
6. **Controlled content:** Approved programme versions, trainer guides, participant materials, change history, and content ownership are documented.
7. **Competence before delivery:** Facilitators complete training, demonstrate practical competence, and receive supervised initial delivery, coaching, and refresher support.
8. **Human responsibility:** Consequential decisions remain with qualified people. Automation does not replace professional or safeguarding responsibility.
9. **Data minimisation:** No identifiable child data is collected without a defined purpose, lawful basis, access model, retention rule, and approval.
10. **Proportionate measurement:** Participation, completion, progress, accessibility, usefulness, workload, and incidents are measured without inventing targets or treating activity counts as impact.
11. **Continuous improvement:** Feedback and implementation evidence lead to controlled revisions rather than uncontrolled local changes.
12. **Honest status and certification:** Namaa does not claim official recognition, effectiveness, readiness, or accreditation without documented authority and evidence.

The detailed training, qualification, safeguarding, data-protection, and permission framework is maintained in [Training and qualification framework](../modules/Training%20and%20qualification%20framework.md).

## 9. Technology and artificial intelligence

Technology and AI are available enabling capabilities, not the definition of Namaa and not an approved child-facing product. Possible future uses include adapting reviewed materials, supporting accessibility, preparing exercises, organising controlled content, documenting implementation, and preparing draft summaries for human review.

The previously proposed internal open-source system and its individual functions remain candidates, not approved requirements. Before any build decision, Namaa must compare the proposed tool with a simpler non-AI process and define the user, problem, data, infrastructure, safeguards, ownership, maintenance, and measurable added value.

AI must not:

- diagnose learning, disability, health, or psychological conditions;
- make safeguarding or referral decisions;
- provide unrestricted medical advice;
- determine a child's educational future;
- operate as an unsupervised child chatbot;
- publish or deliver unreviewed content as approved Namaa material.

## 14. Proposed alternatives for leadership review

The following alternatives are analytical proposals. Namaa project leadership has not selected one for implementation.

| Option | Potential contribution | Main advantage | Main limitation or risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strengthen or fund an existing provider | Improve continuity or accessibility without creating a new delivery structure. | Uses existing capacity and permissions. | Namaa may have limited control over programme design or measurement. |
| Adapt an existing authorised programme | Use existing curricula, remedial models, or life-skills resources. | Lower design burden and possible official alignment. | May not cover the retained programme vision or accessibility needs. |
| Develop one narrow Namaa module with a partner | Test the specialist-development and training model within an existing setting. | Preserves Namaa's contribution while limiting scope. | Requires a suitable partner and clear additional value. |
| Develop a broad standalone Namaa service | Deliver several domains through a dedicated model. | High design control. | Highest duplication, safeguarding, capacity, permission, and sustainability risk. |
| Do not proceed | Avoid exposing children or the association to unjustified risk. | Appropriate if the concept cannot demonstrate responsible value. | The intended support contribution would not be pursued through Namaa. |

No option has yet been selected for implementation.

## 16. Proposed specialist handover package

The following package is proposed for Namaa project leadership approval. It is not yet an accepted handover requirement.

If this dossier is approved for handover, the selected programme team would receive:

- this dossier and the problem/evidence module;
- the [training and qualification framework](../modules/Training%20and%20qualification%20framework.md);
- the selected target group and programme question;
- explicit safeguarding, inclusion, professional-boundary, data, and AI limits;
- the expected review and decision process.

The following specialist return package is proposed:

1. a precise target age and eligibility frame;
2. the evidence-supported need and intended outcome;
3. learning objectives and content boundaries;
4. the proposed delivery and game-based elements, where appropriate;
5. inclusion and accessibility requirements;
6. health, safeguarding, referral, and stop conditions;
7. required facilitator competence and training design;
8. controlled Arabic materials and review responsibilities;
9. a proportionate assessment and quality-improvement approach;
10. data needs, technology options, and a non-AI alternative;
11. dependencies, permissions, risks, and questions for Namaa leadership.

Creating these materials does not authorise delivery. A later, clearly defined gate must review the selected programme, personnel, setting, partner, permissions, safeguards, referral arrangements, data handling, resources, and evaluation plan.

## 18. Proposed next steps and stop conditions

### Proposed sequence for Namaa project leadership approval

1. Namaa project leadership reviews this dossier and either accepts it as the specialist handover baseline or records required changes.
2. Select one first target group and one programme domain.
3. Appoint the necessary subject, adult-learning, inclusion, safeguarding, and Syrian contextual leads.
4. Ask the specialist team to prepare the defined return package.
5. Review the resulting programme before any training, implementation, technology, pilot, or funding decision.

### Stop or defer if

- no competent specialist owner can be appointed;
- the selected programme cannot be distinguished responsibly from existing services;
- safeguarding or referral requirements cannot be met;
- a necessary local host, permission, or professional boundary cannot be established;
- the programme would require unsafe data use or unreviewed AI content;
- available capacity cannot support regular delivery and quality assurance.
