## 2. Short orientation

### Idea

Namaa is a proposed family of non-formal learning and support programmes for children in Syria. It aims to combine educational support, inclusive access, useful life and health knowledge, and developmental opportunities. Qualified specialists would design and review each programme, train Syrian teachers or facilitators, and remain involved in quality assurance. Delivery would take place locally within an accountable and safeguarded framework whose legal form and permission route remain open.

### Long-term vision and intended contribution

Namaa's long-term vision is that children in Syria who need additional learning or support can access safe, professionally developed opportunities that strengthen their capabilities, agency, future prospects, and quality of life. Namaa intends to reach as many children as responsibly possible over time and to contribute, alongside families, schools, professionals, public institutions, and existing organisations, to a better future for children and Syrian society. This is a strategic aspiration, not a current reach, scale, or impact claim.

Namaa's intended added value is to connect knowledge, professional experience, and suitable technologies available in Germany, including experience gained by Syrians living in Germany, with specialist programme development and locally delivered support in Syria. Namaa presents this as a meaningful contribution, not as a complete solution to Syria's educational or wider societal challenges.

### Working problem

Many children in Syria do not receive sufficient, regular, and sound education or additional learning and support opportunities. Namaa's particular design concern is that a reliable, inclusive, and measurable support pathway from access and admission through sustained participation and progress to an appropriate educational connection is not consistently available to every child who needs it. The project does not claim that services are absent or that every child faces the same barrier. Limited reach of existing offers and administrative barriers are retained as contributing concerns; their relative importance and local effect remain unverified.

The reviewed desk evidence is sufficient for this broad problem orientation and for continued concept development. Namaa does not require another general-needs study, a standalone local-validation exercise, or advance ranking of the retained target groups before the specialist phase. This does not establish the suitability, additional value, feasibility, or effect of a particular programme. Those questions become decision-relevant when a programme, participant frame, location, partner, or implementation route is selected. The evidence boundary is documented in [Problem landscape and evidence](../modules/Problem%20landscape%20and%20evidence.md).

### Current phase

Namaa is in concept development. It is not an approved programme, operating service, funded commitment, validated solution, designed pilot, or implemented technology.

### Next consequential decision

## 3. Target-group frame

The retained target groups are listed below. Any barriers described alongside them are evidence-based examples for later specialist consideration, not additional target-group definitions.

1. **Returnee children.** Evidence-reviewed examples include changes in schooling, curriculum, language of instruction, grade placement, or documentation.
2. **Children with disabilities**, including children who need accessible formats, adapted environments, or specialist support.
3. **Children who are not attending school**, irrespective of whether the immediate cause is capacity, poverty, safety, documentation, disability, displacement, or another barrier.
4. **Girls during puberty.** Evidence-reviewed possible concerns include access, health information, safety, family restrictions, and protection.
5. **Children with multi-year education interruptions**, particularly children who missed substantial learning time during the war.

The groups overlap and must not be counted as separate populations. Namaa does not need to rank them against one another before the specialist phase, and a first programme may be relevant to more than one group. Specialists must still define the age, participant profile, eligibility, suitability, and inclusion approach required for the programme they propose. The reviewed desk evidence is not equally detailed for every group; this limitation does not exclude girls during puberty from the retained target-group frame or imply that another group has less need.

## 4. Objectives

Namaa should:

- work over time towards making additional learning and support opportunities available to as many children in Syria who need them as responsible capacity allows;
- strengthen children's capabilities, agency, future prospects, and longer-term life opportunities without claiming that one programme can achieve those outcomes alone;
- give participating children regular and understandable support rather than isolated activities;
- help children develop educational knowledge and skills and remain connected to an appropriate learning path;
- provide useful, age-appropriate knowledge that supports health awareness and practical orientation in life;
- make access and materials inclusive for children with different disabilities and learning needs;
- build local delivery capacity through programme-specific training and continuing support for Syrian teachers or facilitators;
- measure participation, completion, accessibility, progress, usefulness, and implementation quality without inventing impact targets;
- improve programmes through controlled feedback and evidence.

No numerical impact target, cohort size, implementation date, or scale commitment has been approved.

## 5. Programme architecture

Namaa is a modular programme family, not one fixed course. The following are retained candidate domains. Their possible contributions are illustrative questions for specialist review, not settled programme content:

| Domain | Illustrative contribution for specialist review | Current boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| School learning and subject knowledge | Support learning recovery, foundational skills, or continued school participation. | Subjects, age groups, assessment, and sequence must be selected by education specialists. |
| Sport and movement | Support healthy activity, participation, teamwork, and development. | Activities require age-, disability-, safety-, and first-aid review. |
| Coding and digital learning | Develop age-appropriate digital and problem-solving skills. | An academy, platform, device model, or curriculum has not been selected. |
| Physical health and health literacy | Provide simple, accurate, age-appropriate education about bodies, health, hygiene, development, and puberty, including supportive orientation for girls experiencing puberty. | This is non-clinical education. It excludes diagnosis, treatment, therapy, psychotherapy, disease management, and individual medical advice. The specialist team determines the proportionate competence and content review required for the exact topic. |
| Life knowledge and foundational principles | Explore practical understanding, responsibility, relationships, wellbeing, and orientation in everyday life. | Exact topics and principles require transparent specialist and Syrian contextual review. |
| Accessibility and inclusion | Ensure that participation, materials, communication, and assessment work for different needs. | This is a cross-cutting quality requirement, not an optional standalone add-on. |

Game-based and playful learning is a preferred design direction where it supports the learning objective and is appropriate for the child and context. It is not a compulsory method for every programme. Exact pedagogy, content, learning sequence, exercises, and evaluation methods remain the responsibility of the relevant specialists.

## 6. Development and delivery model

The agreed chain is:

> Namaa project concept -> development by qualified specialists -> training of Syrian teachers and facilitators -> delivery with children -> quality assurance and continuous improvement.

The model separates concept ownership from professional content responsibility. Namaa defines purpose, scope, governance, minimum safeguards, documentation, and quality expectations. Specialists determine the professional and pedagogical design within those boundaries. Trained Syrian teachers or facilitators deliver only the programme and version for which they have been prepared and assessed.

Arabic is the default language of child-facing instruction and learner materials. When another language, such as English, is itself the subject being taught, that target language may be used as required by the specialist-approved learning design. The specialist team and Syrian contextual reviewers determine the appropriate Arabic register, terminology, explanatory support, and any bilingual scaffolding.

Remote training from Germany is the current recommended starting model because the concept relies on German-based professional resources and Syrian local delivery. It is not a permanent or approved delivery decision; specialists, the local implementation framework, authorities where relevant, infrastructure, safety, and participant needs may require another model.

## 8. Binding quality principles

Every programme must follow these principles before it can be considered for child-facing delivery:

1. **Specialist ownership:** Content is developed and reviewed by people with documented competence in the relevant subject and age group.
2. **Child safeguarding:** Safe recruitment, a code of conduct, reporting routes, response procedures, referral links, and clear professional boundaries are required.
3. **Syrian contextual review:** Arabic language, cultural context, education pathways, feasibility, and referral arrangements require qualified local review.
4. **Inclusion by design:** Accessible formats and reasonable adaptations are planned from the beginning.
5. **Complementarity:** Namaa must work with or alongside existing services and must not claim a gap merely because public data is incomplete.
6. **Controlled content:** Approved programme versions, trainer guides, participant materials, change history, and content ownership are documented.
7. **Competence before delivery:** Facilitators complete training, demonstrate practical competence, and receive supervised initial delivery, coaching, and refresher support.
8. **Human responsibility:** Consequential decisions remain with qualified people. Automation does not replace professional or safeguarding responsibility.
9. **Data minimisation:** No identifiable child data is collected without a defined purpose, lawful basis, access model, retention rule, and approval.
10. **Proportionate measurement:** Participation, completion, progress, accessibility, usefulness, workload, and incidents are measured without inventing targets or treating activity counts as impact.
11. **Continuous improvement:** Feedback and implementation evidence lead to controlled revisions rather than uncontrolled local changes.
12. **Honest status and certification:** Namaa does not claim official recognition, effectiveness, readiness, or accreditation without documented authority and evidence.

Detailed controls are maintained in the [programme quality framework](../Quality%20Requirements/Programme%20quality%20framework.md), [child safeguarding policy](../Quality%20Requirements/Child%20safeguarding%20policy.md), [monitoring, evaluation and learning framework](../Quality%20Requirements/Monitoring%20evaluation%20and%20learning%20framework.md), and [training and qualification framework](../modules/Training%20and%20qualification%20framework.md). The three Quality Requirements frameworks are provisionally accepted as a working and detailed specialist-review basis. They are not final specialist approvals; the child-safeguarding policy is not adopted by the YISA governing body; and none of these documents authorises implementation.

## 9. Technology and artificial intelligence

Technology and AI are available enabling capabilities, not the definition of Namaa and not an approved child-facing product. Possible future uses include adapting reviewed materials, supporting accessibility, preparing exercises, organising controlled content, documenting implementation, and preparing draft summaries for human review.

The previously proposed internal open-source system and its individual functions remain candidates, not approved requirements. Before any build decision, Namaa must compare the proposed tool with a simpler non-AI process and define the user, problem, data, infrastructure, safeguards, ownership, maintenance, and measurable added value.

AI must not:

- diagnose learning, disability, health, or psychological conditions;
- make safeguarding or referral decisions;
- provide individual medical advice;
- determine a child's educational future;
- operate as an unsupervised child chatbot;
- publish or deliver unreviewed content as approved Namaa material.
