This is a proposed organisation-wide YISA policy drafted in the Namaa repository because Namaa is the first programme for which the safeguards are being designed. Namaa leadership has accepted it provisionally as the working basis for detailed specialist review. It becomes binding only after formal approval by YISA's competent governing body. If adopted, YISA must designate one canonical controlled copy outside any single project repository and apply it consistently across all activities involving or affecting children.

The policy is informed by international standards. It does not establish certification, conformity with a standard, legal compliance, a functioning Syrian referral system, or readiness to work with children.

## 2. Purpose and commitment

YISA is committed to preventing and responding appropriately to harm caused or enabled by its people, partners, activities, communications, technology, data practices, or operations. The best interests, rights, safety, dignity, views, and evolving capacities of each child must guide decisions affecting that child.

Safeguarding is an organisational responsibility. It is broader than reacting to abuse after it occurs. It includes safer recruitment, professional conduct, risk assessment, accessible reporting, appropriate response, partner controls, safe data and communications, competent supervision, and continuous learning.

No programme objective, funding opportunity, organisational reputation, relationship, or deadline takes priority over the safety and rights of a child.

## 3. Scope

For this policy, a **child** is every person under 18 years of age. The policy applies to:

- YISA governing-body members, employees, volunteers, interns, consultants, specialists, trainers, facilitators, visitors, and other representatives;
- Syrian teachers and facilitators delivering or preparing to deliver a YISA programme;
- partners, subcontractors, vendors, and other associated organisations where their role can affect children, to the extent required through due diligence, a written agreement, or an assessed equivalent policy;
- in-person and remote activity, programme design, research and evaluation, fundraising and communications, travel and events, procurement, data handling, and technology or AI use; and
- conduct connected to a YISA role, including conduct outside working hours where it creates a safeguarding risk or undermines suitability to work with children.

The policy covers harm arising through direct contact, online contact, access to information or images, programme design, unsafe omission, abuse of power, or the actions of partners. An internal YISA policy cannot bind an independent partner unilaterally; YISA must apply the required controls through partner assessment and written terms, or accept an equivalent partner system only after documented review. It does not turn YISA, its trainers, or its teachers into a child-protection case-management, medical, psychological, law-enforcement, or investigative service.

This policy is limited to children. UNICEF safeguarding and IASC PSEA frameworks also address harm to adult participants, community members, and personnel. Before operations, YISA must adopt or identify a linked protection from sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (PSEAH), adult-safeguarding, and workplace-harassment route. A concern involving an adult must be transferred safely to that route. YISA must not claim that this child policy alone implements the full UNICEF or IASC PSEA framework.

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Meaning in this policy |

| Child protection concern | Information suggesting that a particular child may be experiencing or be at risk of abuse, neglect, exploitation, or other serious harm, whether or not connected to YISA. |

| Sexual exploitation | Actual or attempted misuse of a child's vulnerability, unequal power, or trust for sexual purposes, including seeking financial, social, political, or other benefit. |

## 5. Governing principles

YISA must apply the following principles:

1. **Equal protection and inclusion.** Every child has an equal right to protection. Risk and barriers may be greater for children with disabilities, girls, displaced or returning children, children outside school, children with interrupted education, and children facing other forms of exclusion.
2. **Best interests and do no harm.** Decisions must consider foreseeable benefits, risks, alternatives, and unintended effects. An activity must not proceed where material risk cannot be reduced to an acceptable and documented level.
3. **Meaningful participation.** Children should receive understandable and accessible information and be heard in decisions that affect them, in ways appropriate to their age, communication needs, and circumstances.
4. **Adult responsibility.** Responsibility for professional boundaries and safe conduct always rests with adults and the organisation, never with the child.
5. **Early reporting without retaliation.** People must be able to raise concerns in good faith without punishment or disadvantage. A report is not an accusation proven true; it triggers safe assessment.
6. **Need-to-know confidentiality.** Information is shared only with people who need it to protect the child, meet a legal or contractual duty, or manage a fair response. Absolute confidentiality must never be promised.
7. **Local relevance with organisational accountability.** Reporting and referral arrangements must fit the selected Syrian setting. YISA remains responsible for how it manages its own people, agreements, and programmes.
8. **Competence and independence.** Triage, referral, investigation, disciplinary action, and specialist support must be handled by people whose competence and independence match the task.

## 6. Governance and accountability

### 6.1 YISA governing body

The governing body must approve the policy, appoint one named Senior Accountable Officer, appoint a governing-body member for safeguarding oversight, appoint a suitably competent Safeguarding Lead and alternate, pre-designate a conflict substitute, provide adequate resources, receive proportionate anonymised assurance reports, and ensure that conflicts of interest do not obstruct reporting or response. Governance oversight, senior executive accountability, and operational safeguarding case handling are separate roles even if a small organisation must allocate more than one role to a person after a conflict assessment.

### 6.2 Senior Accountable Officer

The Senior Accountable Officer holds internal executive accountability for safeguarding implementation, resources, corrective action, escalation to the governing body, and the written safeguarding clearance required for child-facing gates and restarts. The [Programme Quality Framework](Programme%20quality%20framework.md) retains Namaa project leadership as the single default gate owner unless the governing body delegates an exact gate or restart decision to this officer in writing. The role must be named before implementation and must not take over specialist triage or investigation. If the officer is implicated or conflicted, the pre-designated governing-body substitute provides the safeguarding clearance and, where the exact decision was delegated, becomes the decision-maker and must use the independent reporting and advice route.

### 6.3 Safeguarding Lead and alternate

Before any child-facing activity, YISA must appoint a Safeguarding Lead and alternate with written terms of reference, training, protected access to leadership, and authority to require an activity to pause. They coordinate prevention, risk review, reporting, response, local mapping, records, advice, and learning. They do not personally conduct a specialist investigation unless separately qualified and demonstrably independent.

If a concern involves the Safeguarding Lead, the alternate route applies. If it involves both designated roles or senior leadership, an independent external route approved in advance must be available.

### 6.4 Programme leadership

Programme leadership must integrate safeguarding into design, budgets, staffing, technology, procurement, partner selection, delivery, monitoring, and change control. The programme lead cannot accept a safeguarding risk on behalf of the Safeguarding Lead where the latter has identified an unresolved child-safety barrier.

### 6.5 All associated people

Every associated person must understand and follow this policy and the code of conduct, complete required induction, report concerns, cooperate with authorised processes, maintain confidentiality, and stay within the limits of their role. Failure to report or deliberate obstruction may itself constitute misconduct.

### 6.6 Local host and partners

The local host must have named safeguarding responsibilities, workable reporting channels, competent supervision, and a verified local referral annex. Responsibilities must be written into the partnership arrangement. A private individual may contribute as a specialist, trainer, teacher, facilitator, or local contact but cannot alone replace the accountable local structure required for child-facing delivery.

## 7. Safer recruitment and appointment

Every role must be classified before recruitment or appointment according to its safeguarding exposure, including direct or online contact with children, opportunity for one-to-one contact, access to child data or images, authority over children, travel or transport, and influence over safeguarding decisions.

| Role exposure | Examples | Minimum organisational controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limited | No contact with children and no access to identifiable child data | Identity, competence and reference checks proportionate to the role; code and confidentiality duties where relevant |
| Indirect | Designs content, administers systems, or can access information affecting children but has no planned contact | The limited controls plus safeguarding interview questions, role boundaries, data-access controls, induction, and supervision |
| Direct or elevated | Direct, repeated, remote, one-to-one, unsupervised, transport, sensitive-content, or decision-making contact | All above plus enhanced references, legally available and relevant background screening, practical suitability assessment, contact rules, closer supervision, and documented approval before access |

Selection must include a clear role description, identity verification, qualification and experience checks, safeguarding questions, reference checks that address suitability where lawful, conflict-of-interest disclosure, and any legally permitted background check relevant to the role and jurisdiction. No single certificate or criminal-record check proves that a person is safe or suitable. Where a reliable local check is unavailable, YISA must document alternative risk controls rather than pretending that screening occurred. The role-risk classification and suitability review must be repeated when duties, child or data access, location, supervision, delivery method, or contextual risk changes materially.

All appointments must define scope, supervision, confidentiality, data access, reporting duties, intellectual-property arrangements, and consequences for misconduct. The code of conduct must be signed before the role begins.

Visitors and one-off contractors require a proportionate safeguarding protocol even where recruitment controls do not apply. Before access they must receive a briefing or acknowledgement, have a named host, remain escorted or supervised where children are present, have no unsupervised child contact or child-data access, follow image and device restrictions, and leave the activity immediately if the host or safeguarding role requires it.

## 8. Safeguarding code of conduct

All associated people must:

- treat every child with dignity, fairness, patience, and respect;
- use positive, non-violent and non-humiliating approaches;
- maintain professional boundaries in person and online;
- use only approved locations, accounts, devices, and communication channels;
- avoid being alone with a child unless an approved activity design, visibility arrangement, and risk control expressly allow it;
- make reasonable, approved adjustments for disability and communication needs;
- report concerns and boundary breaches through the designated route;
- record only the minimum facts needed and protect all related information; and
- challenge unsafe practice in a way that protects the child.

Associated people must never:

- engage in sexual activity with any person under 18; mistaken belief about age is not an excuse;
- exchange money, assistance, opportunities, goods, services, grades, access, or favours for sexual activity or another exploitative relationship;
- use physical punishment, degrading treatment, threats, intimidation, harassment, discriminatory treatment, grooming, neglect, or emotional abuse;
- develop a sexual, romantic, exploitative, or otherwise inappropriate relationship with a child;
- request or share sexualised content, use sexualised language, or expose a child to unsuitable material;
- favour, isolate, shame, stigmatise, or manipulate a child, including through gifts or secret communications;
- invite a child to a private home, provide private transport, or arrange unapproved meetings or direct messages;
- photograph, record, identify, or publish information about a child without the required purpose, approval, permission, assent, and safety review;
- investigate an allegation, confront an alleged perpetrator, ask leading or repeated questions, or promise secrecy;
- use real child data in training examples, public repositories, or unapproved technology or AI systems; or
- retaliate against a reporter, affected child, family member, witness, or person cooperating with a safeguarding process.

Sexual exploitation or abuse is serious misconduct. Subject to fair process and applicable law or agreement, it is grounds for immediate protective removal from child access and may lead to termination of a role or agreement and external notification. The same protective response applies to another grave breach that creates a serious risk to a child.

### 8.1 Child-to-child safe participation

Each child-facing programme must establish understandable, age- and development-appropriate participation rules for children. These must address respect, inclusion, bullying, discrimination, physical violence, sexualised behaviour, coercion, unwanted contact, taking or sharing images, digital messages, privacy, and how to seek help.

Possible harm by one child to another must be addressed promptly and without stigma. The response must protect all affected children, consider age, development, disability, power differences, intent, repetition, coercion, and the possibility that a child displaying harmful behaviour also needs protection or specialist support. Children must not be investigated, labelled, punished, or managed as adult perpetrators by unqualified facilitators. The Safeguarding Lead must use the approved child-centred procedure and qualified local services where required.

Context-specific rules may be stricter. No local custom or instruction may be used to justify abuse, exploitation, discrimination, or a reduction in a child's fundamental protection.

## 9. Safeguarding risk management

A documented safeguarding risk assessment is required for every programme, location, activity, partner arrangement, event, data flow, communication method, and child-relevant technology. It must consider at least:

- who may be affected and whether age, gender, disability, displacement, schooling status, language, poverty, or another factor changes risk or access to help;
- direct, remote, group, one-to-one, peer-to-peer, transport, venue, sanitation, emergency, and supervision risks;
- sensitive content and the risk of distress, disclosure, stigma, retaliation, or exclusion;
- recruitment, partner, volunteer, visitor, and power-imbalance risks;
- images, stories, devices, accounts, recordings, location data, identifiers, cross-border access, automated tools, and AI;
- the likelihood and potential severity of harm, existing controls, further action, responsible owner, and residual risk; and
- how children, caregivers, local specialists, and persons with disabilities or their representative organisations can inform the assessment safely.

Safeguarding review is required before approval, after a material change, after an incident or near miss, and when the local context changes. If the residual risk cannot be managed, YISA must redesign, pause, relocate, or stop the activity.

## 10. Digital safeguarding, communications, images, and AI

Digital contact is child contact. Before use, YISA must define approved platforms, account ownership, moderation, visibility, participant identity checks where proportionate, group composition, private-message rules, escalation, recording, access, retention, and deletion. Children and caregivers must receive accessible instructions on safe use and how to seek help.

One-to-one private communication between an associated adult and a child is prohibited unless an exceptional programme need is documented, approved, visible to an authorised second adult or organisational account, and covered by a specific risk control. Personal accounts and disappearing-message functions must not be used for child contact.

Images, recordings, stories, or quotations require a defined purpose, risk review, informed caregiver permission where applicable, and age- and ability-appropriate child assent. Refusal must not affect access to the programme. Public representation must preserve dignity, avoid unnecessary identification or location detail, and be withdrawn from future YISA use where the relevant permission is validly withdrawn, subject to clearly explained practical and legal limits.

AI may assist authorised adults with draft preparation or accessibility only under human review. It must not independently interact with children, make safeguarding or referral decisions, infer sensitive traits, diagnose, score a child's future, or process identifiable or sensitive child data without a separately approved ethical, legal, safeguarding, security, and data-protection basis.

## 11. Partners and third parties

Before a partner can participate in child-relevant activity, YISA must assess its safeguarding governance, conduct rules, recruitment, reporting, response, referral capability, data practices, and capacity. The agreement must state:

- the standards and code that apply;
- named responsibilities and escalation routes;
- how concerns are reported and information is shared safely;
- who may commission or conduct an investigation;
- immediate protective and organisational measures;
- support, improvement, monitoring, and audit arrangements;
- data-access and record-control rules; and
- proportionate suspension or termination rights where risk cannot be managed.

A concern does not by itself prove wrongdoing or require automatic termination. YISA must act according to the risk, the child's safety and rights, the available evidence, applicable law and contract, and the partner's willingness and ability to respond safely. YISA must not use partnership arrangements to transfer away its responsibility for its own people or decisions.

## 12. Reporting channels

Before child-facing delivery, YISA and the local host must provide multiple reporting options that are safe, confidential, understandable, accessible to children with different communication needs, available in the relevant languages, and usable without the person complained about. At minimum, the local controlled annex must identify:

1. the YISA Safeguarding Lead and alternate;
2. the local safeguarding focal point and alternate;
3. a child-friendly and caregiver-accessible route;
4. an independent route for concerns involving leadership or designated safeguarding roles;
5. emergency services and competent protection or statutory routes, where safe and applicable; and
6. data-protection and ordinary service-complaint routes so that they are not confused with safeguarding.

The exact Syrian contacts, mandatory-reporting rules, safe authority routes, service availability, and response deadlines are **not verified**. They must be established for the selected location by qualified local safeguarding and legal input before any child contact. No numeric response time is stated here because applicable law, contract, risk, and the local procedure may require different deadlines. Imminent danger requires action without avoidable delay through the safest competent route.

Children and caregivers must be told what can be reported, how, what will happen next, who may receive information, the limits of confidentiality, and that good-faith reporting will not reduce access to the programme.

## 13. Reporting and response procedure

### Step 1 — Receive safely

The person receiving a concern must listen calmly, take the child seriously, avoid blame or leading questions, explain that only people who need to help may receive the information, and avoid making promises about outcomes. The person must not investigate or confront anyone.

### Step 2 — Address immediate safety

If there is an immediate danger or urgent medical need, the receiver must use the approved emergency route and inform the designated safeguarding role as soon as this can be done safely. Actions must avoid increasing danger, retaliation, stigma, or exposure.

### Step 3 — Record and report the minimum facts

The receiver must record, as soon as practicable, what was seen, heard, or disclosed, using the child's own words where possible; distinguish fact from interpretation; and record the action taken. Only necessary identifiers should be used. The record must be sent through the protected route, not ordinary programme chat, email chains, survey data, or the public repository.

### Step 4 — Safeguarding triage

The Safeguarding Lead or qualified delegate must assess immediate risk, conflicts of interest, information needs, applicable duties, safe referral options, and whether the concern relates to:

- possible misconduct by a YISA or partner-associated person;
- a child-protection concern not caused by YISA;
- unsafe programme design or practice;
- sexual exploitation, abuse, or harassment;
- a data or digital-safety incident; or
- an ordinary complaint with no apparent safeguarding element.

Uncertainty must not be resolved by untrained questioning of the child. Qualified advice must be obtained where the route is unclear.

### Step 5 — Protect, support, and refer

Protective action may include changing contact arrangements, restricting access, pausing an activity, arranging safe support, or using a verified external referral route. Support must not depend on whether the child participates in an investigation or whether an allegation is substantiated. The child's views, communication needs, disability, caregiver situation, and risk from disclosure must be considered. A caregiver should be involved when appropriate and safe, not automatically.

Before a non-emergency referral or disclosure of information, the child must receive an understandable and accessible explanation of the proposed recipient, purpose, information to be shared, possible benefits and risks, alternatives, and what may happen next. The child's consent or assent must be sought according to age, capacity, applicable law, and the service involved. Caregiver consent must be obtained where required and safe. Information may be shared without those permissions only where an immediate emergency or a binding legal duty justifies it; the reason, minimum disclosure, protective planning, and qualified advice must be documented.

### Step 6 — Manage organisational misconduct fairly

Where a YISA- or partner-associated person is implicated, YISA must consider neutral interim measures, preserve records, control access, protect all parties from retaliation, and commission an appropriately qualified and independent process. Organisational investigation, statutory investigation, and child-protection case management are distinct. Teachers, facilitators, programme managers, and the initial receiver must not conduct the investigation unless formally appointed, qualified, and free of conflict.

The subject of an allegation must receive fair process appropriate to the decision being made. Safety measures are not a presumption of guilt. Deliberately false or malicious reports may be addressed, but an unsubstantiated or mistaken good-faith concern is not misconduct.

### Step 7 — External reporting and coordination

External notification must follow applicable law, binding funder or partner terms, and the approved local safeguarding and referral annex. Safety assessment informs protective planning and, to the extent the law permits, the method and timing of notification; it does not authorise YISA to ignore a mandatory legal report. Whether a notification may lawfully be withheld or delayed must be determined through applicable law and qualified legal advice, not organisational discretion. A discretionary referral that foreseeably creates greater harm requires a safer alternative and qualified safeguarding judgement. Where duties or risks conflict, YISA must obtain qualified legal and safeguarding advice rather than improvising.

### Step 8 — Close, communicate, and learn

The responsible role must document the decisions, reasons, protective measures, referrals, organisational outcome, remaining risks, and authorised closure. The child and relevant reporter should receive safe, appropriate status information within the limits of privacy and fair process. An anonymised lessons review must identify changes to risk controls, training, supervision, partner management, programme design, or policy.

## 14. Records, confidentiality, and data protection

Safeguarding records must be accurate, dated, tamper-resistant, access-controlled, and separate from routine programme, MEL, and public documentation. Access is strictly need-to-know. Case records and staff records should remain distinct but cross-referenced in a controlled manner where necessary.

Before operations begin, YISA must approve a record structure, responsible controller roles, lawful purposes and bases, access matrix, secure transfer method, breach procedure, retention and deletion rules, and rules for cross-border access. The existence of safeguarding concerns does not permit unlimited collection or sharing. Identifiable case information must not be included in board dashboards, learning reports, training examples, AI systems, or GitHub.

## 15. Training, supervision, and competence

All associated people must receive induction before a child-relevant assignment. Training must match the role and cover professional conduct, recognising a concern, safe first response, reporting, non-retaliation, confidentiality, digital safeguards, inclusion, and the local route. Completion must be recorded and understanding checked; attendance alone is insufficient for roles carrying elevated responsibility.

Syrian teachers and facilitators must be trained to **recognise, respond safely, report, and refer**. They are not trained through Namaa to investigate, diagnose, provide therapy, or manage protection cases. Safeguarding Leads, investigators, and referral or case-management professionals require additional role-specific competence and supervision.

The safeguarding action plan must define proportionate role-based refresher training. Retraining and renewed competence checks are required after a material policy or local-route change, a relevant incident or lesson, a significant role or access change, or evidence that a person does not understand or apply the controls.

## 16. Monitoring, review, and assurance

YISA must maintain a proportionate safeguarding action plan and monitor implementation without exposing case identities. Assurance should cover, at minimum, policy and code acknowledgement, role-risk classification, training, risk assessments, partner readiness, reporting-channel accessibility, response functioning, corrective actions, and unresolved barriers. If this draft is adopted, the governing body should receive a documented safeguarding report and review an updated action plan at least annually, in addition to immediate incident escalation and change-triggered review.

A low number of reports is not proof of safety; it may indicate that reporting is unsafe, unknown, or inaccessible. Children, caregivers, personnel, and partners should be asked—through safe and ethical means—whether they understand the expected conduct and reporting options.

The policy must be reviewed before first child contact, after a serious incident or material programme or legal change, and according to a cycle formally approved by the governing body. Keeping Children Safe includes regular review and an external evaluation or audit every three years in its organisational standard. This is a benchmark for an organisation seeking to demonstrate that it meets that standard, not a general legal requirement or a claim that YISA has already completed such assurance.

## 17. Preconditions and hard stop gates

No Namaa activity involving children, real child data, child observation, child consultation, images, or direct online contact may begin until the appropriate gate in the [Programme Quality Framework](Programme%20quality%20framework.md) is passed for that exact activity. A bounded design consultation or instrument check requires NQ-G0.5; programme delivery requires NQ-G3. At minimum, the following must be documented and approved:

- this policy and the code of conduct have been formally adopted;
- the Senior Accountable Officer, Safeguarding Lead, alternate, governing-body oversight role, conflict substitute, local focal point, and independent reporting route have been appointed;
- access to a qualified independent safeguarding adviser and, if an organisational investigation may be needed, a competent independent investigator or commissioning route has been secured;
- the role-risk classification, safer-recruitment checks, appointment records, induction, and supervision are complete;
- a programme-, activity-, partner-, location-, digital-, and data-specific safeguarding risk assessment is approved;
- accessible child and caregiver information and reporting routes have been tested;
- the local legal, protection, health, psychosocial, disability, emergency, and referral map is current and judged workable;
- partner duties and information-sharing arrangements are written;
- data protection, consent or assent, record access, retention, transfer, and incident arrangements are approved; and
- Namaa project leadership, or another single gate owner formally delegated for that exact decision, has issued a documented decision for the precise programme version, people, place, and delivery method after all required sign-offs, written safeguarding clearance from the Senior Accountable Officer, and written concurrence from the Safeguarding Lead.

Delivery must pause if immediate safety cannot be protected, the reporting or referral pathway stops functioning, a material risk changes without review, prohibited data use occurs, required competent supervision is unavailable, or a responsible safeguarding role directs a pause. Resumption requires a documented incident and risk assessment, verified corrective action, updated local, safeguarding, data, ethics and programme controls, any required external re-review, appropriate support or information for affected people, and written approval by Namaa project leadership as the default gate owner after safeguarding clearance from the Senior Accountable Officer and concurrence from the Safeguarding Lead. A different single decision-maker may act only where the governing body has delegated that exact decision in writing. If the Safeguarding Lead does not concur, the activity remains paused; leadership may commission a new qualified independent assessment but may not simply override an unresolved child-safety barrier. The pre-designated conflict substitute provides the safeguarding clearance or acts as decision-maker, as applicable, where the normal role-holder is implicated.

## 18. Required controlled annexes before implementation

This public policy is not the entire safeguarding system. Before implementation, YISA must create and control:

1. a signed Safeguarding Code of Conduct;
2. a child-to-child safe-participation and response procedure;
3. a role-risk, recruitment, screening, visitor, and one-off-contractor procedure;
4. a confidential reporting and response procedure with forms and access rules;
5. a partner safeguarding standard and due-diligence tool;
6. a digital safeguarding, communications, images, data, and AI annex;
7. a child-friendly and accessible explanation of conduct and reporting;
8. a local safeguarding and referral annex for each delivery setting;
9. a safeguarding action, training, monitoring, and improvement record; and
10. a linked PSEAH, adult-safeguarding, and workplace-harassment route.

The exact local annex is intentionally not completed at concept stage. It cannot be responsibly created until a location, host, programme, responsible people, applicable rules, and real service pathways have been selected and verified.
