This is the detailed quality-control location for Namaa. It turns the quality principles in the project dossier into an auditable programme lifecycle. Namaa leadership has accepted it provisionally as the working basis for detailed specialist review. Its programme-specific requirements remain to be reviewed and completed by the specialist team, and it does not constitute a final policy, professional, legal, programme, training, pilot, or implementation approval.

## 2. Purpose

The framework is intended to ensure that each Namaa programme is relevant, safe, inclusive, professionally developed, contextually appropriate, deliverable by competent people, measurable, and improved through evidence. It applies from concept handover through specialist design, teacher or facilitator preparation, local delivery, review, and any later replication.

It does not prescribe curriculum content, pedagogy, learning objectives, assessment instruments, or success thresholds. Those decisions require qualified specialists for the selected programme, age group, and context. It also does not replace applicable law, authority approval, funder conditions, partner obligations, safeguarding procedures, professional standards, or ethical review.

## 3. Standards hierarchy

Namaa should use a small, transparent hierarchy rather than claiming broad compliance with every international standard:

1. **INEE Minimum Standards, 2024** are the primary education-programme backbone because they cover formal and non-formal education in crisis contexts, including participation, assessment, access, safe learning environments, curricula, teaching, learning assessment, teacher development, personnel, monitoring, and evaluation.
2. **Core Humanitarian Standard, 2024** provides organisation-wide quality and accountability controls for participation, do no harm, complaints, partnerships, learning, competent people, and ethical resource management.
3. **Keeping Children Safe 2024**, UNICEF safeguarding guidance, IASC PSEA standards, and the Child Protection Minimum Standards provide the dedicated safeguarding layer maintained in the child-safeguarding policy.
4. **UNICEF Core Commitments for Children, 2020** provide a child-centred humanitarian cross-check for equitable access, disability and gender inclusion, learning, trained teachers, safe environments, protection, and accountability.
5. **OECD-DAC Evaluation Criteria** and the **UNICEF Policy on Ethics in Evidence Activities Involving People (2026)** inform evaluation questions, evidence quality, ethical review, and claims.
6. **ISO 21001:2025** may later serve as an optional educational-organisation management-system reference. It is not necessary for an initial pilot, the full standard has not been assessed clause by clause, and no certification or conformity claim is made.

The standards are used as reference frameworks unless YISA formally adopts them or a law, authority, funder, or agreement makes particular requirements binding. Namaa may accurately state that its draft controls are **informed by** these sources. It must not state that it is certified, compliant, accredited, or independently verified without the relevant objective evidence.

## 4. Quality objectives

Every released Namaa programme must be able to demonstrate, for its exact version and context, that:

- the defined need and intended contribution are credible and distinguishable from existing provision;
- the target group, eligibility, exclusions, intended outcomes, and professional boundaries are explicit;
- qualified specialists own and approve the content within their competence;
- Arabic language, Syrian context, inclusion, accessibility, age and development, gender, and conflict sensitivity have been reviewed;
- teaching and learning are learner-centred, participatory, non-threatening, and feasible;
- teachers or facilitators have been trained, practically assessed, authorised for the specific programme version, supervised, and supported after training;
- safeguarding, complaints, referral, data, digital, venue, security, and emergency controls work in the selected setting;
- only information needed for a defined decision, duty, or learning purpose is collected;
- participation, delivery quality, proximal outcomes, accessibility, unintended effects, and workload can be assessed credibly and ethically; and
- decisions to continue, adapt, pause, stop, replicate, or scale are documented and proportionate to the evidence.

## 5. Quality status model

Quality status must describe exactly what has been reviewed. One status must never imply another.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Concept draft | An idea or working problem is documented; no specialist or implementation approval exists. |
| Specialist design draft | Qualified specialists are developing the programme; review is incomplete. |
| Specialist-approved programme | The named specialists have approved the defined content, pedagogy, inclusion approach, professional boundaries, and measurement plan for a controlled version. This is not delivery approval. |
| Trainer-ready | The training package and trainer team have passed the training-readiness gate. This does not authorise work with children. |
| Facilitator-authorised | A named person has met the programme-specific competence requirements for a stated version and scope. This is not a general teaching licence. |
| Locally ready | The selected host, place, people, permissions, safeguards, referrals, accessibility, data, resources, and supervision have passed the local-readiness gate. |
| Pilot authorised | Leadership has approved a precisely bounded pilot after all applicable preceding gates. |
| Pilot reviewed | Pilot evidence and limitations have been assessed and a continue, adapt, pause, stop, or limited-replication decision recorded. |
| Independently evaluated | An evaluator sufficiently independent of delivery has assessed a defined scope using documented questions, methods, evidence, limitations, and findings. This is not certification. No such status currently exists. |
| Externally certified or verified | May be used only where the exact standard, scope, recognised verification route, assessor, result, and validity period are documented. No such status currently exists. |

## 6. Quality domains and minimum controls

### QD-01 — Relevance, participation, and complementarity

The broad educational need and retained target-group frame are accepted as sufficiently established for concept development. The selected programme must still define its precise participant frame, intended contribution, relevant alternatives, existing provision, and unresolved assumptions. This programme-specific work is not a renewed requirement to prove the general Syria-wide need or rank the retained target groups. Children, caregivers, Syrian professionals, local actors, and relevant organisations should be involved later where their input is decision-relevant and can be obtained safely, accessibly, and proportionately. Their input must be recorded together with the resulting decision; consultation does not automatically equal approval or representativeness.

The programme must show how it complements, adapts, or strengthens existing education and support pathways and how duplication or displacement will be avoided. A lack of public data is not proof that no service exists.

### QD-02 — Specialist ownership and professional boundaries

Each programme must have named accountable leads whose documented competence matches the subject, age group, pedagogy, inclusion needs, and sensitive content. A Syrian contextual reviewer and an adult-learning or teacher-development lead are required. General health and puberty education remains non-clinical and does not automatically require a medical trainer; the specialist team determines the proportionate competence and review required for the exact content. Psychosocial, child-protection, sport, disability, clinical, or other specialist domains require expertise appropriate to the proposed activity.

The programme must distinguish education and first-line support from diagnosis, treatment, therapy, psychotherapy, disease management, individual medical advice, investigation, formal case management, official certification, and other regulated or specialist activities. Referral triggers and prohibited practices must be explicit.

### QD-03 — Curriculum and content quality

Specialists must define learning objectives, scope, sequence, source basis, age and developmental suitability, Arabic terminology, cultural and conflict sensitivity, diversity and bias controls, connection to the identified need, and compatibility with relevant education pathways. Arabic is the default language of child-facing instruction and learner materials. When another language, such as English, is itself taught, that target language may be used as required by the approved learning design. Specialists and Syrian contextual reviewers determine the Arabic register, terminology, explanatory support, and any bilingual scaffolding. Formal or non-formal alignment and any claim of recognition require the competent authority's documented position.

Materials must have an owner, version, status, review record, authorised uses, translation status, accessibility formats, and change history. Draft or AI-assisted content must not be represented or delivered as approved content.

### QD-04 — Teaching, learning, and assessment

Teaching methods must be learner-centred, participatory, inclusive, confidence-building, and appropriate to age, language, culture, capacity, context, and available technology. Play or game-based learning is used where specialists judge that it serves the learning objective; it is not a universal requirement.

Learner assessment must be tied to what was actually taught, fair, understandable, reliable enough for its intended decision, non-threatening, confidential, and accessible. Appropriate accommodations and alternative ways to demonstrate learning must be available. Children returning after interruption must not be discouraged or punished through assessment. Specialists must define instruments, scoring, interpretation, and the action that results should trigger.

### QD-05 — Inclusion, accessibility, and equity

Inclusion is a design requirement, not an afterthought or an attendance claim. Programme design, information, venue, communication, materials, activities, assessment, feedback, complaints, and data collection must consider disability, gender, adolescence, language, displacement, schooling status, and other relevant barriers.

The design should combine broadly accessible provision with individual reasonable accommodations. Decisions about functional assessment, assistive technology, specialist support, or sensitive characteristics require qualified input and data minimisation. Namaa must not describe a programme as inclusive merely because one or more children with disabilities participated.

### QD-06 — Safeguarding, PSEA, and do no harm

The [YISA Child Safeguarding Policy for Namaa](Child%20safeguarding%20policy.md) is the dedicated control. Before child contact, YISA must have approved governance, recruitment, conduct, risk assessment, reporting, response, referral, partner, digital, data, training, supervision, and assurance arrangements.

Routine feedback, ordinary complaints, safeguarding disclosures, personnel misconduct, and data incidents require distinct routes, records, competencies, and access rights. Teachers and facilitators recognise, respond safely, report, and refer; they do not investigate or manage protection cases.

### QD-07 — Trainer and facilitator competence

The specialist team defines the programme-specific minimum competence, evidence, practical assessment, and authorisation criteria for trainers and facilitators. Namaa leadership applies and documents those criteria. Selection must be based on documented subject competence, adult-learning ability, relevant experience, contextual and language capacity, safeguarding suitability, and the defined role. No universal certificate substitutes for this assessment.

Training must model the approved pedagogy, include practice and feedback, and be followed by a practical competence assessment such as teach-back or observed delivery selected by the responsible specialists. Programme-specific authorisation must identify the person, programme, version, permitted role, assessment result, conditions, and approving role. Attendance alone does not authorise delivery. Initial delivery, coaching, peer support, refresher learning, and wellbeing support must be planned; one-off training without follow-up should be avoided.

### QD-08 — Local delivery readiness

For the selected location, YISA must verify the accountable host, responsibilities, required permissions, curriculum or authority compatibility, suitable personnel, venue and access route, physical and digital safety, accessibility, water and sanitation where relevant, emergency arrangements, reporting and referral pathways, supervision, equipment, connectivity and offline fallback, data flows, insurance, schedule, and adequate resources.

Partner quality review must go beyond safeguarding due diligence. YISA and the host must agree how children and communities participate, how decisions and resources are shared equitably, how each organisation will apply quality and accountability controls, what support or capacity development is needed, how the relationship will be reviewed, and how corrective action or an orderly exit will be managed.

This is not a separate local-validation project during concept development. It is a mandatory go/no-go control once a real host and delivery setting are proposed. The exact Syrian route is not verified and must not be inferred from general standards. Local-readiness evidence must be rechecked before each cohort and whenever the host, personnel, venue, permissions, referrals, security context, delivery method, technology, or data flow changes materially.

### QD-09 — Accountability, feedback, and complaints

Children and caregivers must receive accessible information about the programme, expected conduct, voluntary elements, use of data, feedback, complaints, safeguarding, and what response they can expect. Channels must be safe, confidential where appropriate, accessible, and usable without disadvantage.

Namaa must test whether people know and can use the channels. A low complaint count cannot be treated as proof of quality or safety. Feedback and complaints must be logged, analysed without exposing identities, acted upon, and connected to a visible learning and change process.

### QD-10 — Monitoring, evaluation, learning, and claims

The [Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning Framework](Monitoring%20evaluation%20and%20learning%20framework.md) controls the results chain, ethical evidence gate, indicator design, data quality, analysis, learning decisions, and reporting limits. A small pilot may report reach, participation, implementation fidelity, accessibility, feedback, incidents, and well-defined observed change. It must not claim general effectiveness, national relevance, long-term impact, causation, or sustainability beyond the design and evidence.

### QD-11 — Responsible technology, data, and AI

Technology must solve a defined problem better than a feasible low-tech alternative and work under the actual electricity, connectivity, device, language, accessibility, maintenance, and support conditions. Offline and device-light options should be considered from the start.

Every data element must have a purpose, responsible role, lawful and ethical basis, access rule, security control, retention and deletion rule, and reporting boundary. AI may assist authorised adults with reviewed preparation, accessibility, or administration; it must not replace professional judgement, make safeguarding decisions, diagnose, determine a child's educational future, or use identifiable or sensitive child data without separate approval.

### QD-12 — Resources, continuity, and ethical management

Before delivery, the programme must show adequate people, time, materials, supervision, safeguarding capacity, accessibility resources, contingency arrangements, and responsible financial and asset controls. Volunteer status does not reduce competence, conduct, supervision, duty-of-care, confidentiality, or continuity requirements.

Funding must support the programme's documented purpose and safeguards. Namaa must not weaken scope or controls to fit an unsuitable call, and fundraising communications must not overstate need, readiness, reach, recognition, or impact.

## 7. Programme quality gates

The gates are sequential but may be repeated. Passing one gate authorises only the step stated in that gate. Namaa project leadership is the default single internal accountable owner for every gate unless YISA's governing body formally delegates that exact decision in writing. Required specialist, senior safeguarding accountability, Safeguarding Lead, MEL, data, ethics, and local sign-offs are evidence for the gate, not shared accountability. The Safeguarding Lead may block or pause a child-safety gate. External authority, ethics, funder, or partner approvals are mandatory evidence where applicable but do not replace YISA's internal decision record.

### NQ-G0 — Concept handover readiness

**Decision:** May the concept be handed to appointed specialists for programme development?

**Minimum evidence:** current project dossier; problem and evidence boundary; retained target groups and candidate domains; scope and non-scope; role boundaries; quality, safeguarding, data, AI, and claim limits; open assumptions and dependencies; and the proposed [specialist task and return package](Specialist%20team%20handover%20tasks.md).

**Decision owner:** Namaa project leadership.

**Possible outcomes:** proceed to specialist design; proceed with stated conditions; revise; defer; stop.

**Boundary:** This gate does not select a programme automatically and does not authorise training, research with children, data collection, funding claims, or delivery.

**Current status:** The concept handover basis was accepted through DEC-009 and updated through CHG-001 in [Project Dossier 0.4](../core/Project%20dossier.md). The identified specialist group's formal role allocation and assignment record remain pending. No later gate has been passed.

### NQ-G0.5 — Bounded child participation or instrument-validation readiness

**Decision:** May children participate in one precisely defined design consultation, comprehension or accessibility check, or instrument-validation activity before full pilot readiness?

**Minimum evidence:** a clear need that cannot reasonably be met through adult, specialist, or fictional-data review; bounded protocol and participant frame; local accountable host; applicable authority position; child-safeguarding risk assessment and reporting or referral route; ethics-and-risk triage and any required independent review; accessible caregiver information, permission and child assent; data-minimising plan; trained personnel; distress and stop rules; support and follow-up; and written limits on use of findings.

**Required sign-offs:** Senior Accountable Officer for safeguarding, Safeguarding Lead, MEL or research lead, data-protection role, relevant specialist, Syrian contextual reviewer, local accountable lead, and independent ethics reviewer where triggered.

**Decision owner:** Namaa project leadership.

**Possible outcomes:** authorise the exact bounded activity; authorise with conditions; use adult or non-child alternatives; defer to NQ-G3; stop.

**Boundary:** This optional gate does not authorise programme delivery, general child research, recruitment to a pilot, or reuse of child data. If it is not passed, direct child participation must be recorded as deferred and must not be claimed.

### NQ-G1 — Programme release

**Decision:** Is one defined programme version professionally ready to become the basis for trainer preparation and later local-readiness work?

**Minimum evidence:** selected target and eligibility frame; needs and complementarity analysis; theory of change; learning objectives; curriculum and source map; Arabic and Syrian contextual review; pedagogy and assessment plan; inclusion and accessibility plan; professional-boundary and referral-trigger matrix; safeguarding and ethical risk review; trainer competence requirements; controlled materials; MEL and data plan; technology rationale and non-digital fallback where relevant.

**Required reviewers:** accountable subject lead, adult-learning lead, inclusion specialist, safeguarding specialist, Syrian contextual reviewer, and any domain-specific professional required by the content.

**Decision owner:** Namaa leadership on a documented specialist recommendation.

**Possible outcomes:** specialist-approved; conditionally approved for specified revision work; revise; defer; stop.

### NQ-G2a — Trainer, training-package, and adult-cohort readiness

**Decision:** May the approved training package be delivered to the defined adult cohort by the named trainers?

**Minimum evidence:** trainer dossiers; trainer competence and safeguarding suitability; role-risk classification and completed checks; signed appointments and code; specialist-approved training package and assessment rubric; participant criteria; applicable written local authorisation for the adult training; data, platform, confidentiality, accessibility, insurance, schedule, support, and contingency arrangements; prohibition or separately gated control of child contact and real child data; supervision and wellbeing plan; and accurate certificate wording.

**Required sign-offs:** accountable subject lead, adult-learning lead, Safeguarding Lead, Syrian contextual reviewer, local accountable lead, and data-protection role where personal data will be processed.

**Decision owner:** Namaa project leadership.

**Possible outcomes:** adult training authorised for the exact package, trainers, cohort, and method; authorised with pre-start conditions; revise; defer; stop.

**Boundary:** NQ-G2a does not authorise a trainee to deliver to children.

### NQ-G2b — Named facilitator authorisation

**Decision:** Has each named teacher or facilitator demonstrated competence and suitability for a precise Namaa role and programme version?

**Minimum evidence:** verified identity and appointment status; completed role-risk assessment and checks; training attendance and completion; practical teach-back or observed competence evidence; assessment result and conditions; safeguarding and professional-boundary competence; signed code; programme-specific authorisation record; initial delivery supervision, coaching, refresher, wellbeing and escalation plan; and accurate certificate wording.

**Required sign-offs:** accountable subject lead, adult-learning lead, and Safeguarding Lead. A local supervisor must confirm availability and acceptance of the supervision role before NQ-G3.

**Decision owner:** Namaa project leadership.

**Possible outcomes:** authorised for a precise role, version, setting type, and period or review trigger; authorised with conditions; further development required; not authorised.

**Boundary:** Completion or participation certificates are not state teaching qualifications and do not alone authorise child contact.

### NQ-G3 — Local delivery and pilot readiness

**Decision:** May the exact programme version be delivered by the named people, with the named host, at the named place and through the proposed delivery method?

**Minimum evidence:** capable local host and signed roles; partner quality and accountability assessment; equitable decision and resource arrangements; partner support, relationship-review and corrective-action plan; required written authority positions or permissions; current safeguarding and referral annex; tested reporting routes; programme and location risk assessment; safe and accessible venue and emergency arrangements; verified staff availability and supervision; caregiver and child information; documented ethics-and-risk triage for all evidence activity and any independent review triggered by the MEL framework; consent and assent process where applicable; data-management and transfer controls; tested materials, equipment, connectivity and offline fallback; implementation schedule and adequate resources; insurance and other required operational confirmations.

**Required sign-offs:** Senior Accountable Officer for safeguarding, Safeguarding Lead, programme specialist lead, MEL Lead, data-protection role, Syrian contextual reviewer, and local accountable lead, plus documented external approvals where applicable.

**Decision owner:** Namaa project leadership.

**Possible outcomes:** bounded pilot authorised; authorised with explicit pre-start conditions; revise; defer; stop.

**Hard boundary:** No child contact, real child data, observation, consultation, image collection, or delivery before this gate is passed for the exact scope, except a separate activity expressly authorised through NQ-G0.5.

### NQ-G4 — Pilot review

**Decision:** What does the evidence justify after or during the bounded pilot?

**Minimum evidence:** participant flow; delivery and fidelity record; accessibility and accommodation record; staff competence and supervision evidence; learning or proximal outcome data; feedback and complaint accessibility; safeguarding, incident, near-miss, and data summaries without unnecessary identifiers; costs and workload; deviations and changes; data quality, missingness and attrition; context; unintended effects; analysis limitations; specialist and local interpretation.

**Required sign-offs:** programme, safeguarding, MEL, and local leads. Outcome analysis should be quality-checked by a competent person who was not responsible for routine delivery.

**Decision owner:** Namaa project leadership.

**Possible outcomes:** continue within the same bounded scope; adapt and re-review; pause; stop; prepare limited replication evidence; commission stronger independent evaluation.

**Boundary:** A positive pilot review is not automatic approval to scale and is not proof of causal impact.

### NQ-G5 — Replication or material change

**Decision:** May the programme be used in a new setting or after a material change?

**Triggers:** new location, partner, target group, age range, language, curriculum domain, sensitive topic, trainer model, delivery method, technology, data flow, authority context, or significant learning from an incident or evaluation.

**Minimum evidence:** impact analysis on every affected domain; updated specialist and contextual review; revised safeguarding, referral, ethical, data, accessibility, resource, and MEL controls; change log; versioned materials; and repeated local-readiness evidence.

**Required sign-offs:** every specialist, safeguarding, MEL, data, local, and external role required by the affected earlier controls.

**Decision owner:** Namaa project leadership.

**Possible outcomes:** approve the new bounded version; approve with conditions; return to an earlier gate; defer; stop.

## 9. Controlled evidence packages

Namaa should keep the system proportionate through six controlled packages rather than duplicating information across many files:

1. **Quality scope and standards register:** applicable references, editions, status boundaries, owners, review dates, and any binding contractual requirements.
2. **Programme design and release dossier:** the evidence required for NQ-G1, including specialist approvals and controlled materials.
3. **People and competence files:** roles, selection, checks, appointments, training, assessments, authorisations, supervision, and conditions.
4. **Safeguarding, complaints, ethics, and data pack:** policies, risk assessments, routes, local annexes, decisions, access rules, and assurance records.
5. **Local delivery-readiness dossier:** host and partner quality assessment, equitable roles and resources, permissions, site, personnel, referral, technology, data, resources, schedule, insurance, relationship review, corrective-action arrangements, and gate decision.
6. **MEL and change record:** theory of change, indicator definitions, tools, analysis plan, delivery and outcome data, deviations, learning, decisions, and version changes.

Identifiable child, personnel, safeguarding, complaint, health, disability, or other confidential records must be held in an approved restricted system, never in the public repository.

## 10. Deviations, corrective action, and change control

A deviation is any departure from an approved programme version, role, procedure, condition, or gate. The person who identifies it must protect immediate safety, record the minimum necessary facts, and notify the responsible lead. The responsible roles must determine whether to correct immediately, adapt under controlled authority, pause, escalate as a safeguarding or data incident, return to an earlier gate, or stop.

Corrective action must identify the issue, cause where reasonably ascertainable, affected people and versions, immediate containment, responsible owner, required action, completion evidence, and whether effectiveness must be checked. A document update alone is not evidence that a corrective action worked.

After a baseline, material changes require a change record with the trigger, reason, affected evidence and risks, specialist and local review, approval, new version, communication and retraining needs, and any requirement to re-establish measurement comparability. Local improvisation is not permitted where it changes learning objectives, sensitive content, safety, eligibility, assessment, data, or professional boundaries.

## 11. Quality monitoring and management review

Quality monitoring should be light enough to operate reliably but sufficient to detect unsafe, inaccessible, ineffective, or uncontrolled delivery. It should review programme status, gate conditions, participation and attrition, fidelity, competence and supervision, accommodations, feedback and complaints, incidents and near misses, data quality and breaches, resources and continuity, deviations, corrective actions, and unresolved dependencies.

Leadership review must result in documented actions rather than a dashboard alone. The review should record what evidence was considered, limitations, the decision, responsible owner, conditions, and next review trigger. Serious safeguarding or safety issues are escalated immediately and never wait for a routine review meeting.

## 12. External communications and claims

Every external claim must match the evidence and status. Unless later evidence supports more, Namaa should use wording such as:

> This draft Namaa framework is informed by the INEE Minimum Standards (2024), the Core Humanitarian Standard (2024), and international child-safeguarding and evaluation guidance. This does not constitute adoption, implementation, certification, accreditation, legal approval, or a claim of conformity.

Namaa must not claim that:

- a programme is approved merely because a draft or specialist review exists;
- a facilitator is a licensed teacher because Namaa issued a certificate;
- a programme is safe because no concern was reported;
- a programme is inclusive because it admitted some children with disabilities;
- activity counts prove learning or impact;
- observed change was caused by Namaa without an appropriate design; or
- one cohort, place, or module establishes effectiveness for children in Syria.
