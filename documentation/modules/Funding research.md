## Starting Point: Namaa and YISA's Position at the Research Review Date

Namaa was conceived as a locally delivered learning and support offer for children in Syria, potentially beginning with a small pilot in Jaramana. The draft combined learning support, age-appropriate health and everyday-life information, and inclusive, low-barrier access. The proposed first steps were a needs assessment, partner review, limited pilot, and evaluation. A digital or AI-supported component was envisaged only as a supervised internal tool, not as an autonomous child-facing chatbot, diagnostic system, or safeguarding decision system.

Funding eligibility depended significantly on organisational matters that had not yet been documented as complete at the research review date. Registration, tax-recognised charitable status, an association bank account, and operational funding and Syria-payment processes had not been fully verified in the available YISA documents. A confirmed Syrian implementation partner was also missing. Larger grants were therefore considered realistic mainly through experienced partners or at a later stage.

In addition, a JRS centre supported by Kindermissionswerk already operated in Jaramana, providing education, tutoring, psychosocial support, and children's-rights activities. Before submitting an application, Namaa would therefore need to demonstrate a specific service gap and a complementary role in relation to existing provision. Early coordination with JRS or other local providers was considered important both professionally and strategically for funding.

## Broad Search: Routes Reviewed but Not Included as Robust Namaa Options

- **Qatar Fund for Development – Expression of Interest:** The official page refers to registered organisations, a minimum project volume of USD 1.5 million, at least 50% co-financing, and implementation capacity in the target country. However, the countries and themes currently available for selection are visible only in the portal; an open Syria focus is **Not verified**. It was therefore not considered a robust Namaa option. [Official EOI page](https://www.qatarfund.org.qa/expression-of-interest/)
- **International Islamic Charity Organization – Funding Portal:** A portal exists for accredited field partners, and previous educational work for Syrian refugees and internally displaced people is documented officially. A current public call, funding amount, and deadline are **Not verified**. It was therefore not included in the assessment. [Official evidence of Syria and education work](https://www.iico.org/en/news-ar/international-islamic-charity-organization-and-islamic-development-bank-..-a-new-educational-partnership-to-support-forcibly-displaced-people-ar.html?currency=BHD)
- **Erasmus+ Small-scale Partnerships:** Although this is a suitable entry format with lump sums of EUR 30,000 or EUR 60,000, Syrian organisations cannot participate as partners in this format, and it is therefore not the appropriate funding route for local implementation in Syria. It was not included as a Namaa Syria-project option.
- **EU Funding & Tenders Portal:** At the research review date, no open or announced call specific to Syria and Namaa's themes could be robustly identified that was directly accessible to a young association. This does not mean that no future call will emerge.

## 1. The Five Most Realistic Funding and Support Pathways

1. **The Pollination Project – Daily Grant:** At the research review date, this offered the lowest formal entry threshold and could support a very small co-design exercise, material test, or needs test. Staffing, budget, reference, and payment requirements must be confirmed first.
2. **BMZ Small Projects Fund for Development Cooperation (F10):** The strongest route for an initial substantive Syria pilot. It nevertheless requires completion of YISA's legal, tax, and financial setup, reliable financial processes, child safeguarding, and an independent Syrian partner, normally with at least one year of cooperation.
3. **startsocial – Consulting Fellowship (Beratungsstipendium):** Not conventional project funding, but especially realistic for a young association seeking to build funding readiness, roles, finances, impact management, and partnerships systematically before the pilot. The next round must be awaited.
4. **Stiftung RTL – Wir helfen Kindern:** Strong thematic alignment and, according to the snapshot, potentially approachable during 2026, but only with verified charitable status and an experienced on-the-ground structure. The funding amount and acceptance of a Syria project must be clarified in advance.
5. **Erasmus+ KA220-YOU:** The most realistic larger European route if YISA joins an experienced consortium as a junior partner and Namaa is designed as a transnational education, methods, and capacity-building project rather than exclusively as a local service-delivery model.

**Close alternatives:** Kindermissionswerk funding is strongly aligned thematically but requires a suitable local organisation and clear differentiation from, or cooperation with, the already funded JRS service in Jaramana. House of Resources Berlin could support the next local organisational and diaspora phase but does not finance the Syria pilot.

## 2. YISA's Most Important Missing Prerequisites at the Research Review Date

1. **Completed legal and tax capacity:** A current register extract, a board authorised to represent the association, and a tax-exemption notice or other reliable evidence of charitable status.
2. **Active financial infrastructure:** An association bank account, dual-control principle, budget approvals, receipt and voucher management, fund-drawdown procedures, and processes for exchange rates and Syria-related payments.
3. **An independent, legally registered Syrian partner:** Its own leadership, bank account, and accounting system; local permissions; due diligence; and documented experience working with children. F10 normally requires at least one year of partnership.
4. **Evidence of the local service gap:** A structured needs and stakeholder analysis in the intended community, particularly in relation to the existing JRS service in Jaramana; documented additional value instead of a parallel structure.
5. **Adopted child-safeguarding system:** Binding policies for YISA and its partner, a code of conduct, safe complaint and reporting channels, referral arrangements, consent procedures, and clear responsibilities.
6. **A fundable pilot plan:** A clearly defined age group, location, cohort, duration, content, staffing, budget, theory of change, baselines, indicators, and stop criteria.
7. **Data protection and safe technology:** Data minimisation, consent, role-based access, retention and deletion, an incident process, and clear boundaries for digital and AI-supported tools.
8. **Professional quality assurance:** Qualified education, inclusion, health, and child-safeguarding specialists; review of Arabic-language and accessible materials.
9. **Documented experience and references:** Small completed activities, documented outcomes, independent references, reliable reports, and, where possible, an initial external evaluation.
10. **Legal and operational Syria review:** Local registrations and permissions, sanctions, money transfers, counter-terrorism and anti-money-laundering checks, procurement, insurance, and security. The specific design is **Not verified** and requires specialist review.

## 3. Possible Types of Partner Organisations

- **A registered Syrian child, education, or youth organisation** in the intended area, serving as an independent local implementing organisation.
- **A school, education directorate, municipality, or other competent public body**, particularly if rooms, equipment, or social infrastructure form part of the pilot.
- **An organisation of persons with disabilities or a local inclusion and accessibility specialist**, ensuring that accessibility is not addressed only in theory.
- **A child-safeguarding and referral partner** with safe reporting pathways and links to qualified protection, health, and psychosocial services.
- **A licensed health or psychosocial specialist organisation** to review health-literacy content professionally, without turning Namaa into a treatment service.
- **An experienced German or European lead organisation** for F10, Stiftung RTL, Erasmus+, or a potential ECHO sub-partnership, including grant-application, financial-management, and compliance experience.
- **JRS and other providers already operating in Jaramana**, to clarify needs, referral pathways, and complementarity and to avoid duplicate structures.
- **A university or monitoring and evaluation partner** for the baseline, pilot design, accessible evaluation, and credible evidence of impact.
- **A Berlin-based diaspora, school, or global-learning partner** for the Berliner Landesstelle für Entwicklungszusammenarbeit (LEZ), House of Resources, or Erasmus+ components in Germany.
- **An open-source, Arabic-language accessibility, or education-technology partner** for a narrowly defined digital support component with a secure data architecture.
