# Namaa Training and Qualification Framework

- **Version:** 0.4
- **Research review date:** 1 August 2026
- **Responsible role:** Namaa project leadership
- **Current project phase:** Exploration and concept development

## Purpose and status

This document defines a preliminary organisational, qualification, safeguarding, and regulatory framework for training Syrian teachers and facilitators to deliver future Namaa programmes with children. It translates the current project model into an operationally understandable structure:

> Namaa project concept -> development by qualified specialists -> training of Syrian teachers and facilitators -> delivery with children -> quality assurance and continuous improvement.

The framework does not determine the educational content, teaching methods, learning sequence, or programme architecture. Those decisions remain the responsibility of the relevant education, health, inclusion, child protection, sport, or technology specialists. It also does not authorise training, work with children, travel to Syria, data processing, programme implementation, or the issue of a state-recognised qualification. Each of those steps requires a separate readiness decision based on the facts applicable at that time.

## Recommended starting model

The most proportionate starting model is free, non-formal professional development for adult teachers and facilitators in Syria, delivered remotely from Germany and supported by qualified Syrian professionals. Namaa should not initially present this training as a German or Syrian state-recognised teacher qualification. This keeps the purpose clear: the training prepares participants to deliver a defined Namaa programme under Namaa's quality and safeguarding system; it does not grant a general teaching licence or a regulated professional status.

For this narrowly defined model, the research did not identify a general German state licence that a person must hold merely to provide non-formal adult training. Individual specialists may therefore contribute as volunteers or other formally appointed persons if Namaa verifies their competence and defines their responsibilities in writing. A German teaching qualification is not automatically required. The German Instructor Aptitude Ordinance, commonly associated with the AdA or AEVO certificate, applies specifically to trainers responsible for recognised vocational occupations under German vocational training law and does not by itself govern the proposed Namaa training. See [Section 1 of the German Instructor Aptitude Ordinance](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ausbeignv_2009/__1.html).

The fact that Namaa's specialists work voluntarily is not, on its own, decisive for German distance-learning law. The relevant question is whether participants receive a contract-based, paid distance-learning course with spatial separation and monitored learning progress. A genuinely free internal programme for Syrian participants is unlikely to require approval by the German State Central Agency for Distance Learning under the current model. This conclusion must be reviewed if participation later becomes paid, if a funder effectively purchases individual course places, or if the contractual and assessment model changes. See [Section 1 of the German Distance Learning Protection Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fernusg/__1.html).

## Who should be permitted to train

Namaa should distinguish legal permission from internal suitability. The absence of a general state trainer licence does not mean that any available person should train teachers. Each trainer should demonstrate both relevant subject expertise and the ability to train adults. Namaa should verify identity, qualifications, professional experience, references, prior teaching or facilitation experience, language capacity, and any conflicts of interest before appointment. Each trainer should also sign a written role description, confidentiality undertaking, intellectual-property agreement, safeguarding code of conduct, and acknowledgement of the limits of the assigned role.

For every selected programme, the responsible specialist team defines the proportionate minimum competence profile and evidence for each trainer and facilitator role, together with the practical assessment and programme-specific authorisation criteria. Namaa leadership verifies the submitted evidence and records the formal appointment or authorisation. An initial group with education and child-support experience exists, but its detailed composition, competence coverage, responsibilities, availability, and formal assignment are not documented in this public framework.

Subject expertise should match the programme being developed. School learning and catch-up content should be led by qualified education professionals. Inclusive education should involve specialists with demonstrable experience in disability inclusion and accessible learning. General health and puberty education does not make trainers or facilitators healthcare providers and does not automatically require a medical trainer. The specialist team must ensure that the information is accurate, age-appropriate, source-based, and within Namaa's non-clinical boundary, and must determine the proportionate review competence needed for the exact topic. Any proposed clinical, illness-specific, diagnostic, therapeutic, or individual-advice element would fall outside the current concept and require reassessment and appropriate professional involvement. Psychosocial and child-protection content should be led by professionals whose competence matches the intended intervention. Sport and movement content should involve appropriately qualified sport or movement professionals, while coding and digital-learning content should combine technical expertise with educational expertise.

Health and psychosocial content requires explicit professional boundaries. Within the current Namaa concept, teachers may be trained to provide simple, age-appropriate information about health, bodies, development, hygiene, and puberty and to use approved support and referral instructions. They must not diagnose, investigate, treat, manage disease, provide psychotherapy, or give individual medical advice. If a later proposal crosses into professional medical or psychotherapeutic practice, it must be separately reassessed and may require appropriately authorised professionals; see the [German Non-Medical Practitioners Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/heilprg/__1.html) and the [German Psychotherapists Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/psychthg_2020/__1.html).

Each programme should have an accountable subject lead, an adult-training or teacher-development lead, and a qualified Syrian contextual reviewer. One person may fulfil more than one role if their competence is documented. Syrian involvement is necessary to review language, cultural relevance, practical feasibility, referral pathways, and consistency with the applicable education context. This review is a professional responsibility and does not transfer Namaa's organisational accountability to an individual trainer.

## Qualification and certification approach

There is no universal international Train-the-Trainer licence that would, by itself, make a person suitable for Namaa. External trainer certificates, adult-education qualifications, first-aid certificates, recognised sport qualifications, and safeguarding courses can provide useful evidence, but they do not replace subject competence, background checks, practical assessment, supervision, or local authorisation.

Namaa should establish its own limited internal qualification process. Before a Syrian teacher or facilitator is authorised to deliver a particular Namaa programme, the person should complete the relevant training, demonstrate the programme through a practical teaching exercise, show that they understand safeguarding and referral procedures, and receive documented feedback. A first delivery should be supervised or observed, followed by periodic coaching and refresher training. Internal authorisation should always refer to a particular programme and version; it should not be treated as a general licence to teach or support children.

Three different forms of evidence must remain distinct. A **Certificate of Participation** confirms attendance only. A **Certificate of Completion** may confirm that defined training and assessment requirements were completed, but it must not imply a state teaching qualification. **Accredited Professional Development** may be claimed only where the competent education authority has expressly recognised the programme. Until such recognition is documented, Namaa should use only the first two forms and include a statement such as:

> This is a non-formal Namaa training certificate. It does not constitute a state-recognised teaching qualification or professional licence.

The certificate should identify the programme, version, training hours, assessment status, issuing organisation, responsible trainers, and issue date. Attendance alone must not automatically authorise work with children.

## Syrian authorisation and institutional coordination

The applicable route in Syria depends on who participates and where the later programme is delivered. Training independent facilitators through a locally authorised civil-society organisation is likely to be the most manageable non-formal route. Public-school teachers, state training centres, school premises, alignment with the national curriculum, or any claim of officially recognised professional development require prior written coordination with the competent Syrian education authorities.

Current public information indicates that the Syrian Ministry of Education is strengthening national and subnational coordination of education programmes and international partnerships through the Local Education Group and its training structures. See the [Syrian Arab News Agency report on the Local Education Group](https://sana.sy/en/education/2327811/). The exact application forms, approval sequence, trainer criteria, processing times, and authority that may recognise a Namaa certificate were not established through published official material. **Not verified.**

Namaa should therefore continue concept and training-system development without treating the missing procedural detail as evidence that no approval is required. Before the first training cohort, the local implementing organisation should obtain a project-specific written response from the relevant education directorate and, where applicable, the Ministry of Education and the competent social-affairs or humanitarian-coordination body. The submission should describe the programme's non-formal status, participants, training format, learning objectives, trainers, Arabic materials, assessment method, safeguarding system, data handling, intended certificate, and proposed delivery setting.

Individual teachers and other private persons may act as participants, trainers, facilitators, or local contacts. They should not, by themselves, replace an authorised local host organisation, a documented safeguarding structure, or the responsible education authority where official teachers, public premises, or recognised certification are involved.

## Safeguarding and suitability to work with children

Safeguarding must be built into trainer selection, programme design, training delivery, local implementation, supervision, and incident response. A detailed [YISA Child Safeguarding Policy for Namaa](../Quality%20Requirements/Child%20safeguarding%20policy.md) has now been drafted from current international standards. It remains unadopted and does not yet contain the verified local reporting and referral annex required before child contact. Its controls include a binding code of conduct, safeguarding risk assessment, safer recruitment, designated safeguarding roles, accessible reporting routes, response procedures, partner controls, digital safeguards, and local implementation gates.

German specialists who train adults and have no access to children do not automatically require an extended German certificate of conduct solely because they provide the training. If their role creates direct, recurring, or comparable contact with children, Namaa should conduct a risk-based assessment and normally require the appropriate background evidence. German statutory requirements can also arise in child and youth welfare arrangements depending on the nature, intensity, and duration of contact; see [Section 72a of Book VIII of the German Social Code](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_8/__72a.html). Equivalent local screening for Syrian personnel should be required to the extent that reliable procedures and records are available. The exact Syrian screening mechanism is **not verified**.

Teachers and facilitators should learn how to recognise a concern, respond safely, record only what is necessary, and use the designated referral route. They should not investigate allegations, promise confidentiality that cannot be maintained, confront an alleged perpetrator, or attempt diagnosis or therapy. Sensitive modules must not begin until a realistic local escalation and referral pathway exists.

## Data protection and remote delivery

The simplest and safest training model avoids identifiable child data altogether. Training exercises should use fictional or properly anonymised cases. Children should not normally appear in live training sessions, and sessions should not be recorded by default. If an exceptional need for recording is established, Namaa must define the purpose, legal basis, access restrictions, retention period, deletion process, and information provided to participants before recording begins.

When the German organisation processes personal data, it must define lawful purposes, minimise the data collected, restrict access, establish deletion periods, and allocate responsibilities between Namaa, trainers, technology providers, and local partners. Health, disability, safeguarding, and similar information requires particularly careful treatment. The governing European requirements are set out in the [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng).

If personal data under Namaa's responsibility is made accessible to an organisation or service provider in Syria, the rules on international data transfers must also be assessed. Syria does not currently appear on the European Commission's [list of countries with an adequacy decision](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en). Standard Contractual Clauses may form part of an appropriate transfer mechanism, but they require an assessment of the actual transfer and, where necessary, supplementary safeguards. See the European Commission's information on [Standard Contractual Clauses](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/standard-contractual-clauses-scc_en). Avoiding child data and unnecessary cross-border access should remain the default design choice.

Remote training should be designed for unstable connectivity and limited access to devices. Materials should be available in low-bandwidth and offline formats, and the training process should not depend on continuous video participation. These operational choices support access, but they do not replace safeguarding, qualified personnel, supervision, or local approval.

## Required training package

Before specialists train the first cohort, each programme should have a controlled package containing defined learning outcomes, a statement of scope and prohibited uses, an approved trainer guide, child-facing instruction and participant materials in Arabic by default, accessible and offline alternatives, practical exercises, an assessment rubric, safeguarding and referral instructions, data-protection instructions, a certificate template, and clear rules for adaptation. When another language, such as English, is itself taught, that target language may be used as required by the specialist-approved learning design. The package should identify its version, responsible subject lead, reviewers, approval status, language status, and next review date. Namaa should also hold documented rights to use, translate, adapt, and distribute the materials for the intended purpose.

The training itself should combine explanation with practical demonstration, participant practice, feedback, and a teach-back or comparable competence assessment selected by the responsible specialists. Completion should be followed by supervised initial delivery, structured feedback, and continuing support. The [INEE Minimum Standards on Training, Professional Development and Support](https://inee.org/minimum-standards/standard-14) emphasise qualified trainers, context-appropriate professional development, recognition by education authorities where certification is claimed, and ongoing support rather than isolated workshops.

Namaa should maintain a trainer dossier, participant and assessment records, programme-version register, incident and complaint records, approval correspondence, supervision records, and a change history proportionate to the risks involved. Access to safeguarding and personal records must be restricted; not all records belong in a public repository.

## Additional operational controls

Volunteer appointments, confidentiality, intellectual-property rights, safeguarding duties, supervision, and liability should be documented in writing. Namaa should confirm that its organisational liability and volunteer insurance arrangements cover the actual remote or international activity before delivery. Relevant persons and organisations should also be screened against the remaining targeted EU restrictive measures applicable to Syria. The EU lifted broad economic sanctions in 2025 but retained targeted measures against listed persons and entities, renewed until 1 June 2027; see the [Council of the European Union decision summary of 18 May 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/18/syria-council-renews-restrictive-measures-targeting-the-former-al-assad-regime-for-one-year-and-de-lists-certain-entities/).

Remote delivery is the recommended starting assumption, not a permanent project decision. If German specialists are later expected to travel to Syria, entry and work-permission requirements, insurance, security management, emergency arrangements, and organisational duty of care must be assessed separately. As of the research review date, the German Federal Foreign Office continues to warn against travel to Syria and states that consular assistance is extremely limited. See the [current Syria travel and security advice](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/laender/syrien-node/syriensicherheit-204278).

## Open issues and implementation gates

The following matters remain open and should be resolved only when they become relevant to an authorised implementation step:

- the exact Syrian approval route for the selected local organisation, participants, locations, and programme;
- the authority and criteria for any desired Syrian recognition of professional development;
- the available and reliable Syrian background-check mechanism;
- the local safeguarding, health, psychosocial, and emergency referral network;
- the final allocation of data-controller and processor responsibilities;
- the applicability of German distance-learning approval if payment, contracting, or assessment arrangements change;
- insurance coverage for the selected delivery model; and
- entry, work authorisation, and security arrangements if on-site training is later considered.

Before training begins, Namaa project leadership should apply the training and facilitator readiness controls in the [Programme Quality Framework](../Quality%20Requirements/Programme%20quality%20framework.md) and confirm that the trainer-selection process, programme package, local written authorisation, safeguarding system, data-protection arrangements, technology, insurance, assessment model, supervision capacity, and certificate wording are ready. Before work with children begins, the separate local-delivery and pilot-readiness gate must confirm the local host and team, venue, permissions, safeguarding and referral capability, approved programme version, supervision, data and MEL arrangements, resources, and permitted scope.

## Current recommendation

Namaa should continue preparation on the basis of a free, non-formal, remote-first training model for adults. The identified specialist group should first define programme-specific competence criteria and complete its role and competence mapping. Namaa should then appoint or authorise people against those documented criteria, require safeguarding and programme-specific qualification, assess teachers and facilitators through practical demonstration, issue only accurately described Namaa participation or completion certificates, and obtain written Syrian authorisation before the first cohort. Formal accreditation may be considered later, but it should not delay development of a robust internal training and quality framework.

This recommendation is a planning position. It is not a legal opinion, regulatory approval, curriculum approval, pilot approval, or authorisation to begin delivery with children.
